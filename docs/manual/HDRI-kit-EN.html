How to shoot and stitch an HDRI

From the first frame on location to checking cast shadows and chrome reflections in Blender. Drag frames, compare shutter stops and convert ND filters directly on the page.

1

Photographing the environment

Where to put the camera

Place the lens where your CG subject will sit. For a kettle on a kitchen counter, hold the camera roughly halfway up the kettle so chrome and glossy materials reflect the counter and window from the real viewpoint.

Pick a marker on the floor straight beneath the lens. Shooting handheld, keep the phone over that point and step around it yourself. Holding the phone at arm’s length and twisting at the waist swings the lens in an arc: the counter shifts against the window frame, leaving a double edge when aligned.

Choosing a camera and support

A phone works well when you only have a couple of minutes on set. Pick one lens, disable automatic lens switching if your app allows it and keep the originals. An ultrawide covers the sphere faster, though indoors you should check the corners for softness; in dim light, the main lens with extra frames is cleaner.

On a manual camera, lock focus, focal length, white balance, ISO and aperture, changing only shutter speed across the bracket. A tripod buys time for noise-free long shadow exposures; a standard head keeps the horizon steady, though its pivot sits behind the lens.

Setting the no-parallax point on a panoramic rail

In a tight room, line up a near vertical edge against a distant one (such as a chair back over a window mullion). Pan left and right while sliding the camera along the rail until both edges stay locked together. Re-check after changing lenses or zoom.

Covering the sphere

Start on a clear landmark, shoot a level row and include that same landmark again at the end to close the loop. Leave roughly a third of each frame overlapping the next, with stationary detail inside the shared strip. If the overlap holds only empty sky, tilt down slightly to catch a roofline.

Shoot tilted rows above and below with vertical overlap, plus the zenith overhead and the nadir beneath the lens. A clean floor shot taken after moving the tripod aside supplies real texture for reflections — keep the lens as close to the original spot as you can.

Windows, lamps and direct sun

Complete the whole exposure bracket in one direction before turning. Start on a normal exposure, shorten the shutter until the brightest important light reveals detail, then lengthen it for deep shadows. With ISO and aperture locked, one stop (1 EV) doubles or halves shutter time.

For 3D lighting, shoot a RAW or DNG bracket whenever possible. A gain-map HEIC stores extra display HDR headroom rather than full scene range. Standard JPEGs and HEICs are already camera-processed — usable as backdrops, yet if a window clips white in every frame, exporting to EXR cannot bring back its intensity.

Shooting direct sun through an ND filter? Write down the attenuation in stops and the frame numbers for the editor. Move swiftly around drifting clouds or foliage, and wait for a clean frame if someone walks across a key join.

Your first panorama

Load and inspect your frames

Open Create, click the + bubble and select every photo taken from that spot. On Mac you can drop files straight into the window. HDRI kit reads JPEG, HEIC, PNG, TIFF and supported RAW files. Prefer originals, since messaging apps strip exposure and lens metadata.

Show Photos

Opens the frame grid so you can remove stray shots from another room. On Mac press Space to Quick Look a shot; the cross removes it from the set, and Done closes the sheet on compact screens.

Clear

Empties the current selection before starting another shoot. Original files on disk stay untouched.

Choose a quality setting and stitch

Choose Fast to check coverage. Leave Maximum selected for the final panorama.

Stitch Panorama

Requires at least two photos. The Editor opens once stitching finishes: check that the 360° loop closes and zoom into a doorway or obvious join. If stitching stops, Back to Photos returns to the set.

3

The editor

Inspect the panorama

Once stitching finishes, the Editor opens with three top-level views: the 2:1 Flat projection, inside-out Sphere navigation and path-traced Render lighting over an 18% grey ball, chrome ball and floor.

Flat

Displays the unwrapped 2:1 sphere. Pinch to inspect a join and drag to pan. Double-click or double-tap selects a source frame so dragging adjusts its placement; click empty space or press Deselect to resume panning.

Sphere

Look around from the capture point. Drag to turn and pinch to zoom. Tilt straight up and straight down to spot gaps near the poles.

Show All · Rotate · Pause · Reset View

Show All refits the flat map after zooming. In Sphere view, Rotate starts automatic panning, Pause stops it and Reset View restores the viewing camera.

Render

Path-traced lighting preview: use the 18% matte ball for light direction and softness, the chrome ball for reflections and the floor for cast shadows. Toggle each object independently; drag to orbit, pinch to zoom and double-click to reset.

Fill Window · Fit 2:1

Fill Window uses all available space; Fit 2:1 brings the full 2:1 rectangle cleanly back into view.

Auto Proxy

Temporarily lowers preview resolution during edits and restores full detail as soon as you release. The number below displays current preview width in pixels.

Show Panel · Hide Panel

In a compact window or iPhone landscape, collapse the side panel to examine a seam unobstructed. In portrait layout controls sit below the preview. On touchscreens, Done finishes an action and Cancel exits point picking.

Frames and positions

Bracketed exposures facing the same direction are grouped into “positions” (stacks) automatically. List filters, locks and restitch commands sit in the first tab of the side panel.

All · Positions · Exposures

All lists every frame flat. Positions groups bracketed shots facing the same direction; Exposures groups frames by brightness. Click a position header to pick its reference frame. On Mac, Space opens Quick Look and arrow keys step through images.

Link Exposures · Unlink Exposures

The lock links placement tweaks across every exposure in a position. Unlock it if a single shot shifted. A master control below the list toggles all positions. Drag a misgrouped frame onto its target position or choose Separate into Own Position.

Hide from Panorama · Return to Panorama · Delete Frame

The eye temporarily excludes a frame from blending; press again to restore it. Delete Frame removes it from the project while leaving the original on disk. At least two frames must remain.

Add Photos and Restitch

Adds frames to the open panorama and recalculates alignment. Restitch Without Hidden rebuilds using only enabled frames. A recovery snapshot is saved beforehand; re-check alignment and masks afterwards.

Adjusting a frame and ND

Double-click any frame on the flat panorama (or pick it from the list) to open its alignment inspector: yaw, pitch, roll, scale, ND attenuation and the sun-bracket toggle.

Yaw · Pitch · Roll · Scale

Yaw slides the frame along the horizon, pitch tilts up or down, roll spins around the viewing axis and scale adjusts projection size. Anchor on a stationary detail in the overlap and click any value for numeric entry.

Quick Turn · Reset · Hide

The 90° buttons roll the frame left or right; one 180° button flips it upside down and the other points it behind the camera. Reset restores the computed pose; Hide closes the inspector and clears selection.

ND Filter / EV Shift

Enter attenuation in stops or choose a preset (ND8 passes 1/8 of the light, or 3 stops). No Filter clears the shift. You can also assign an ND preset to an entire group from its header menu.

Sun Bracket

Tags dark ND shots of the light source so HDRI kit blends them only where ordinary exposures clipped. An inaccurate filter strength directly shifts solar brightness in the exported EXR.

Stitch Check · Check · Check Again

Measures misalignment across overlapping detail in pixels of an 8K panorama. Open a high-error frame, check the join against its reported neighbour and re-run after adjusting. Pairs without shared features have no measurable error.

Scene colour and orientation

Scene changes the exported file; View changes only the screen

White balance, north yaw and horizon levelling in the Scene tab are written directly into your exported EXR and HDR. Sliders in the View tab act strictly as a display monitor.

White Balance · Temperature · Tint · Eyedropper

Changes the colour of the exported map. Sample a grey card or unclipped neutral surface with the eyedropper (sampling a tinted wall introduces an opposite colour cast). Reset restores as-shot balance at 6504 K and 0 tint.

Pick Center · North to Center

Click the direction to place at the centre of the map, or centre north from the compass estimate. If compass readings scatter, pick a landmark or type an exact yaw angle above. Rotation carries through to export.

Horizon · Two Points

Adjust pitch and roll numerically, or click two points along the true horizon roughly 60–120° apart. A distant shoreline works well; a nearby tabletop does not. Reset clears the levelling adjustment.

Retouching ghosts, seams and the tripod

When a pedestrian walks through a frame, wind moves foliage or tripod legs sit at the nadir, open Retouch for deghosting, per-position brush masks and nadir patching.

Remove Ghosts · Graph-Cut Seams

Zoom in on a doubled detail such as a passing pedestrian and compare ghost removal on vs off. If a seam still shows, try Graph-Cut Seams to reroute the cut across the overlap and inspect neighbouring details.

Exclude · Force · Erase · Brush Radius

Select a frame from the target position. Paint Exclude over an unwanted object where a neighbour holds clean background; Force favours the selected position; Erase clears strokes locally. Brush Radius is in degrees; Clear Mask of Position wipes all strokes for that stack.

Fill the Nadir · Texture · Smooth · Tripod Radius

Choose the smallest radius that covers the tripod. Texture borrows patches of surrounding ground; Smooth extends edge colours gently. Inspect floor tiles in Sphere view. Filled pixels are flagged synthetic; radius 0 fills only the uncaptured pole.

View adjustments, channels and LUTs

Monitoring tools for inspection on screen: stop exposure, channel isolation, clipping and gap overlays, false colour, reference ACES view transforms and custom .cube LUTs.

Exposure · Contrast · Highlights · Shadows · Gamma

Preview-only monitoring controls. Exposure shifts brightness in stops, Contrast separates tones and the histogram displays brightness distribution.

Temperature · Tint · Saturation · Reset Grading

Inside View, these sliders change only the screen image. For white balance in the exported file, use Scene. Reset Grading restores neutral preview settings.

Clipping and Uncaptured Areas · False Color (Stops)

Select RGB, a single colour channel or luminance (L). Clipping marks full saturation in red, partial channel clipping in orange and uncaptured areas in violet stripes. False Color maps brightness into stops around middle grey.

View Transform

On SDR displays, compare Standard (sRGB), ACES 1.3 SDR, ACES 2.0 SDR and Khronos PBR Neutral: Standard clips bright highlights to display white early. ACES 2.0 HDR supports EDR displays; Raw displays untouched linear values. None alter the file.

Load .cube LUT… · LUT Input · Remove LUT

Load a .cube file and enable it by name. Display applies it after the SDR view transform; ACEScct and Log2 send the matching log signal into an output LUT replacing the standard transform — check your LUT’s specification. Disabling keeps it saved; Remove LUT deletes it.

4

Projects and settings

Returning to a shoot

Every stitched panorama autosaves with its frames, masks and adjustments. Projects lets you reopen past shoots immediately, relink moved originals and reclaim cache space.

Open · Rename… · Show in Finder

In Projects, click a card to reopen an autosaved panorama directly in the Editor. Extra actions live in the context menu on Mac or the “•••” menu on touchscreens. Show in Finder opens the project folder on Mac.

Copy Photos into Project · Find Missing Photos…

Reconnect an external drive before exporting linked projects, or copy photos into the project to keep it self-contained. After moving originals, run Find Missing Photos and pick their folder (unique filename matches only), then reopen the project.

Retry · Cancel

If a project save fails (such as low disk space), fix the cause and press Retry. Cancel dismisses the alert — verify edits saved before closing.

Project Storage · Clear Cache

Shows disk usage for photos and cache. Cache holds processed frames HDRI kit can rebuild anytime: clearing it keeps photos and recovery copies intact, though the next full open may take slightly longer.

Recovery Copies… · Restore as New Project · Delete Project

Pick a snapshot saved before a restitch and restore it as a separate project to compare results. Confirming Delete Project also removes packed photo copies — confirm your originals exist elsewhere first.

5

Checking and exporting the map

Reading the report

The HDR report translates photometry into plain diagnostics: it lists source types, flags clipped light sources and estimates the ND attenuation needed to capture them cleanly.

Report · Details

Lists source types, measured brightness range and the fraction of the sphere clipped across every exposure. Bracket range comes from EXIF + ND (capture sequence), measured range describes the assembled map. Brightness is relative without lux calibration; RAW clipping is estimated from developed frames.

Show Clipped Area · Show Clipping on the Panorama

Show Clipped Area jumps to a flagged source; Show Clipping enables the overlay across the map. Click −2, −6 or −12 EV to dim preview exposure (click again for 0 EV). If a lamp turns into a uniform grey patch, check the warning and shortest exposure: it needs a darker shot.

Violet stripes at the bottom indicate missing ground — supply a clean floor frame or patch the nadir and tell the recipient about the synthetic fill. Doubled straight edges mean heading back to Stitch Check: wide EXR range and a level horizon alone do not confirm a clean join.

Choosing the output file

Clean default for Blender Environment Texture. Keep masks off; switch Pixel Type to Float (32-bit) when the report shows peaks above 65,504.

Export… · Format · Resolution

Use EXR for 3D lighting and compositing, Radiance HDR (.hdr) for RGBE software and 16-bit PNG for flat SDR output. Widths are 2K, 4K, 8K and 16K in strict 2:1 proportion. Start at 2K for a quick test, then size for reflection sharpness.

Color Space

Start with Linear Rec.709 for general-purpose environment maps. Pick ACEScg or Linear Display P3 when your receiving pipeline expects it. PNG offers sRGB and Display P3. Always share the colour space you chose, especially for wide-gamut exports.

Pixel Type · Compression

For EXR, use Half (16-bit) when peaks stay below 65,504 and Float (32-bit) for higher solar values. ZIP compresses losslessly in 16-line blocks, ZIPS works line by line, PIZ suits grainy frames and Uncompressed gives the largest file.

Masks (mask.saturated, mask.synthetic)

Adds compositor diagnostic channels for pixels clipped across the whole bracket and synthetic nadir/fill regions. Leave masks off for Blender environment textures; export a second masked copy if your compositor needs them.

Review the summary and choose Export. On Mac pick a filename and folder; on iPhone or iPad wait for preparation and choose a location in Files. If full-resolution loading fails, reconnect the source drive or run Find Missing Photos from the project menu. If memory runs tight, lower the resolution.

Testing the EXR in Blender

In the Shader Editor switch from Object to World. Add an Environment Texture node, open your EXR, select Equirectangular and connect Color → Background → World Output. Match the texture’s Color Space to the space you exported and begin with Strength at 1.0.

Place a matte grey sphere and a polished chrome sphere on a ground plane. Mute other lamps so only the environment lights the scene, and switch Cycles to Rendered. Read light direction and crispness from the shadow on the plane, and inspect horizon alignment and seams in the chrome reflection. If the panorama also acts as a visible backdrop, judge resolution through the final camera.
